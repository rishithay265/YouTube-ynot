### Description (paste as is)
The Kubera Yantra is a 3×3 array of the integers 20–28 whose rows, columns and main diagonals all sum to 72; in South India it is also drawn in rice flour as the kubera kolam. Most of what is said about it in popular literature is unsourced. This paper traces the deity and the ritual attached to the yantra through primary texts, from the Atharvaveda (8.10.28) and the Vaiśravaṇa rite of the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka (1.31) to the Rāmāyaṇa, the Mahābhārata (12.221), the Amarakośa, the Kulārṇava Tantra, the Mantramahodadhi (1588 CE) and the Dharmasindhu. It locates the widely chanted 35-syllable Kubera mantra and its meditation verse at Mantramahodadhi 16.105–109, and the Ekapiṅgala episode at Rāmāyaṇa 7.13.21–30. It then proves, with exhaustive machine checks, that the Kubera square is the Lo Shu reflected top-to-bottom and translated by 19, and that among the 114 essentially distinct 3×3 magic squares with sum 72 it is the only one built on consecutive integers. Varāhamihira's perfume square (Bṛhatsaṃhitā 76) and the Khajuraho square are re-verified as pandiagonal, and the 384 pandiagonal squares of order four are recounted. Finally, the grid is mapped onto the eight quarters under several drawing conventions, and the tradition's own account of how the yantra draws and keeps wealth is reconstructed from its premises. Each claim is graded as textual fact, scholarly interpretation or hypothesis; no causal efficacy is claimed. Code and full computational output are included in this record.

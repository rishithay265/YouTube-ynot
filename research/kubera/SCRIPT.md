# The Kubera Code · कुबेर यंत्र का रहस्य: script for approval

*Hidden in Plain Sight, episode 1.* Long-form, about 13 minutes. English narration (voice to be chosen); Sanskrit chanted by Vāgdhenu.
Every factual line is anchored to `VIDEO_BRIEF.md`, the paper (`paper/kubera_yantra.pdf`) and the saved passages in `excerpts/`. The source for each section is in its heading.
The voice is the tradition's own: we explain the yantra the way the śāstras read it. Where a link is our own discovery, the narration says so plainly ("a pattern hidden in plain sight"), so nobody can say we put words in a rishi's mouth.

Visual notes in *[brackets]*. Everything is drawn in code (SVG); no generated images.

## S1 · Seventy-two  (Kuberakolam; code/magic3.py)  ≈ 1:20

*[Black screen. A hand-drawn rice-flour line glows into a 3×3 grid at a doorstep. Numbers appear one by one.]*

- Every morning, at doorsteps across South India, someone bends down and draws nine numbers in rice flour.
- Twenty-seven. Twenty. Twenty-five. … Twenty-two. Twenty-four. Twenty-six. … Twenty-three. Twenty-eight. Twenty-one.
- Add any line. Across. Down. Corner to corner. … Seventy-two. … Every single time.
- This is the Kubera Yantra: the seat of the god who guards the treasures of the three worlds.
- No ancient book writes these nine numbers down. … They have lived in people's hands, passed from floor to floor, mother to daughter, for generations.
- And inside them, something is hidden. … Take nineteen away from every number…
- *[Cells tick down: 27→8, 20→1 … the grid flips top to bottom.]*
- …and a square appears that China says rose out of the Luo river, on the back of a turtle.
- Tonight we decode the Kubera Yantra the way the tradition itself reads it. … The god. The square. The mantra. The rules. … And the logic of how it is said to draw wealth, and keep it.

*[Title card: THE KUBERA CODE · Hidden in Plain Sight]*

## S2 · The god of what is hidden  (AV 8.10.28; ŚB 13.4.3.10; TA 1.31.1, 1.31.5)  ≈ 2:00

*[Starfield. A cosmic cow drawn in gold line; a calf beside her.]*

- To understand the yantra, start with the god. … And the oldest Kubera is not the one you think.
- In the Atharvaveda, the cosmic cow, Virāj, goes to the hidden folk. … Her calf is Kubera Vaiśravaṇa. … And what they milk from her is *tirodhā*. … Concealment.
- Concealment: the power to put things out of sight.
- Now listen to the word for treasure: *nidhi*. … *Ni-dhā*: to set down, to put away. … And concealment, *tiro-dhā*: to put out of sight. … The same root sits in both: *dhā*, to place.
- In the Veda's own language, treasure is wealth that has been put away. … And Kubera is the god of what is kept hidden.
- The Śatapatha Brāhmaṇa calls him King Kubera Vaiśravaṇa, … and says his people are the Rakṣas.
- The Taittirīya Āraṇyaka gives him a whole sacrifice of his own. … It begins: *Concealment was born from the waters.*
- And before anything is asked for, the first ball of rice is offered to a strange guest: … *your obstructing brother, who lives in my heart, … so that he does not destroy my aims.*
- Remember this brother. … He comes back at the end.

## S3 · The guardian who asked for nothing  (Rām. 7.3.14–18, 7.11, 7.13.21–30, 7.15.29; Amarakośa)  ≈ 2:00

*[An ascetic in tapas; years pass as rings of light. Brahmā appears on a lotus.]*

- Vālmīki tells how this god rose. … In the Uttara Kāṇḍa, Vaiśravaṇa, son of the sage Viśravas, sits in tapas for thousands of years: … living on water, then on air, then on nothing at all.
- Brahmā appears and offers him a boon. … And Kubera does not ask to be rich.
- He asks for two things: … to be a guardian of the world, … and *vitta-rakṣaṇam*: the protection of wealth.
- Not to own it. … To guard it.
- Brahmā makes him the fourth lokapāla, beside Indra, Yama and Varuṇa, … and gives him the flying chariot, Puṣpaka.
- *[Laṅkā in gold. A shadow crosses it.]*
- He rules Laṅkā. … Then his half-brother Rāvaṇa demands it. … And their father, Viśravas, tells Kubera: leave. Go to Kailāsa. … Kubera obeys.
- Later Rāvaṇa takes the Puṣpaka too.
- On Kailāsa, during his vow, Kubera's left eye falls on Pārvatī, … and her radiance scorches it yellow.
- Śiva, pleased with his eight-hundred-year vow, makes him a friend. … From that day he is Ekākṣipiṅgala: the one with the single yellow eye.
- *[A list of names writes itself in Devanagari and IAST.]*
- The Amarakośa, the oldest Sanskrit thesaurus, keeps his names. … Tryambaka-sakha: Śiva's friend. … Ekapiṅga. … And Śrīda: the giver of Śrī, … the giver of Lakṣmī.
- It names his nine treasures, the nava nidhi: … Mahāpadma, Padma, Śaṅkha, Makara, Kacchapa, Mukunda, Kunda, Nīla, Kharva.
- And it seats him in the North.
- Nine treasures. … Nine cells. … Hold on to that.

## S4 · The code inside the square  (code/magic3.py; Bṛhatsaṃhitā 76; Khajuraho; Navagraha yantras)  ≈ 2:30

*[The grid returns, large. Lines light up one at a time with running totals.]*

- Now the square itself. … Why is it built this way?
- Nine numbers in a row, twenty to twenty-eight. … Together they make two hundred and sixteen. … Three rows share it equally: seventy-two each.
- And the centre has no choice. … It must be twenty-four: exactly one third of seventy-two. … In every square like this, the middle holds a third of the whole. … The centre is the point of balance.
- Now the astonishing part. … Take the numbers one to nine. … There are three hundred and sixty-two thousand, eight hundred and eighty ways to arrange them in a grid.
- *[A counter races to 362,880; eight survivors glow.]*
- Only eight are magic. … And all eight are the same square, turned or flipped. … In all of mathematics, there is only one.
- So Kubera's yantra is that one square, raised by nineteen. … Subtract nineteen and you are left with eight, one, six; three, five, seven; four, nine, two: … the Lo Shu of China, turned upside down.
- In India the same pattern belongs to the Sun. … In the Navagraha yantras, each planet has its own version: … the Sun's lines add to fifteen, the Moon's to eighteen, Mars to twenty-one, and on through the nine.
- Kubera's is that same pattern lifted to seventy-two. … And there are a hundred and fourteen different magic squares whose lines add to seventy-two. … Only one is made of nine numbers in a row. … Kubera's.
- *[A 4×4 perfume square; drops of colour fill cells.]*
- India was building such squares more than fourteen centuries ago. … Varāhamihira, in the Bṛhat Saṃhitā, wrote a perfume recipe on a four-by-four square, … where every line, even the broken diagonals, adds to eighteen.
- *[A temple wall at Khajuraho; the square carved in stone.]*
- On the wall of the Pārśvanātha temple at Khajuraho, a square is carved where every row, every column, every diagonal, … and every little block of four, adds to thirty-four.
- These were never doodles. … They were recipes, temple walls, … and seats for the gods.

## S5 · The compass  (code/directions.py; Amarakośa; Lo Shu)  ≈ 1:30

*[The grid lifts off the floor and turns; compass letters N, E, S, W appear around it.]*

- Now place the yantra the way its god would want it: … facing his own direction, North.
- Put the top row to the north. … The north cell holds twenty. … Take away the nineteen… … one.
- And in China's map of this square, the north is one: … water. … The south cell here is twenty-eight: … nine, … and nine is China's south: … fire.
- Mirror east and west, … and all nine cells line up with the Chinese compass.
- Two civilisations. … One square. … One compass.
- And one more pattern, hidden in plain sight. … Write Kubera's nine treasures across the numbers, in the order the Amarakośa gives them. … The fifth treasure lands in the centre, on twenty-four, the heart of the square. … Its name is Kacchapa: … the tortoise.
- *[A tortoise shell outline settles over the centre cell.]*
- The square China says came on a turtle's back … holds a tortoise at Kubera's heart.
- No scripture tells us this. … It is simply there, … waiting to be seen.

## S6 · The breath of the yantra: mantra and rules  (Kulārṇava 6.85–87; TA 1.31.6; MM 16.105–111, 20.1–14; Mantra Mahārṇava; Dharmasindhu)  ≈ 2:30

*[The grid in a copper dish; a lamp is lit.]*

- But a yantra is not just a drawing. … The Kulārṇava Tantra says: … the yantra is to the deity what the body is to the soul. … Worshipped in the yantra, the deity is pleased at once.
- A body needs breath. … That breath is the mantra.
- The oldest comes from the Veda itself: the Taittirīya Āraṇyaka. … It is chanted at the end of pūjās to this day, in the Mantrapuṣpam.

> **CHANT (Vāgdhenu):** राजाधिराजाय प्रसह्यसाहिने । नमो वयं वैश्रवणाय कुर्महे । स मे कामान्कामकामाय मह्यम् । कामेश्वरो वैश्रवणो ददातु । कुबेराय वैश्रवणाय । महाराजाय नमः ॥
> *[Words light up karaoke-style with IAST under each.]*

- *To the king of kings, who overcomes all by his might, … to Vaiśravaṇa, we bow. … May he, the lord of desires, grant my desires to me who desires. … To Kubera, son of Viśravas, the great king: homage.*
- The japa mantra comes from the Mantramahodadhi, a manual of mantras completed in 1588, … in its sixteenth wave.

> **CHANT (Vāgdhenu):** ॐ यक्षाय कुबेराय वैश्रवणाय धनधान्याधिपतये धनधान्यसमृद्धिं मे देहि दापय स्वाहा ॥

- *Yakṣāya*: to the king of the Yakṣas. … *Kuberāya*: to Kubera. … *Vaiśravaṇāya*: to the son of Viśravas.
- *Dhana-dhānya-adhipataye*: to the lord of wealth and of grain. … *Dhana-dhānya-samṛddhiṃ me*: abundance of wealth and grain, to me.
- *Dehi*: give. … And then, *dāpaya*: … cause it to be given. … Let it come to me through the world.
- Thirty-five syllables. … With Oṃ, thirty-six: a perfect Bṛhatī, the metre the text itself assigns.
- And look who the text names as the seer of this mantra: … Viśravas. … Kubera's own father, … the father who once told him to give up Laṅkā.
- The text gives his form for meditation:

> **CHANT (Vāgdhenu):** मनुजवाह्यविमानवरस्थितं गरुडरत्ननिभं निधिनायकम् । शिवसखं मुकुटादिविभूषितं वरगदे दधतं भज तुन्दिलम् ॥

- *Worship the round-bellied lord of treasures, … seated in a fine chariot borne by men, … shining like the garuḍa-gem, … friend of Śiva, crowned and adorned, … giving boons and holding a mace.*
- *[Checklist cards slide in, each with its source in small type.]*
- And it gives the rules. … One lakh japa, with ten thousand offerings of sesame into fire. … Or ten thousand japa in a Śiva temple, for increase of wealth. … Or one lakh japa sitting at the root of a bilva tree.
- The Mantra Mahārṇava gives the installation: … the yantra placed in a copper dish, anointed with ghee, bathed with milk and water, … set in an auspicious maṇḍala, … and given life through prāṇa-pratiṣṭhā, … then worshipped in a hexagon, inside a square of guardians.
- Where no material is named, the Mantramahodadhi says: write it on birch bark, silk or palm leaf, … and seal it in gold, silver or copper.
- The Vedic rite is done at every new moon and full moon. … And the Dharmasindhu says to write Kubera's grain mantra and keep it where the grain is stored.
- On Dīpāvalī night, the same text says, lamps are lit and Lakṣmī is worshipped, … and after midnight, Alakṣmī, misfortune, is driven out of the courtyard.
- And to this day, families across India worship Kubera beside Lakṣmī on Dhanteras and Dīpāvalī, facing north.

## S7 · Lakṣmī's conditions, and the logic of the yantra  (MBh 12.221; MkP 68; TA 1.31.5)  ≈ 2:10

*[A palace of the asuras, lit, then dimming. Lakṣmī as a lotus of light, never a figure.]*

- But wealth itself is not Kubera's to create. … Śrī is Lakṣmī. … And in the Mahābhārata, she tells Indra why she once left the asuras.
- She lived with them while they were truthful and generous. … When their conduct turned, she left.
- And she says exactly what she cannot live with. … Houses left unswept. … Sleeping past sunrise. … Eating fine food alone. … Grain left scattered for crows and rats. … Admiring wealth that was earned wrongly.
- The Mārkaṇḍeya Purāṇa describes the owner of the Śaṅkha treasure: … he eats well while his own family eats poor rice. … Wealth that has stopped flowing.
- *[The grid returns. Each step of the chain lights a cell, ending at the centre.]*
- Now put it all together, the way the tradition does.
- One: Lakṣmī chooses where she lives, by how people live.
- Two: the treasures serve her.
- Three: Kubera guards them. … He asked to protect wealth, not to own it.
- Four: the yantra is his body, the mantra his breath, … and prāṇa-pratiṣṭhā brings him in.
- Five: the square is a small cosmos. … Every direction equal, at seventy-two. … The centre steady. … Nothing leaks.
- Six: facing north, keeping the counts, the places and the days the texts prescribe, … the devotee stands in line with the guardian.
- Seven: … the obstructing brother who lives in the heart is fed first. … The Veda already knew where the real blocker lives.
- So this is how the tradition says the yantra works. … Lakṣmī comes through worship and conduct. … She stays because Kubera guards, … and because we stop driving her away.

## S8 · Hidden in plain sight  (AV 8.10.28; Rām. 7.3.14)  ≈ 0:50

*[Back to the doorstep. Dawn. The rice-flour grid, quiet.]*

- The oldest Kubera was a god of concealment. … Treasure, in the Veda's own words, is wealth that has been put away.
- And the yantra on the floor does not shout. … Every line equal. … The centre still. … It keeps.
- Kubera never asked to be rich. … He asked to guard.
- Maybe that is the code. … Lakṣmī brings. … Kubera keeps.

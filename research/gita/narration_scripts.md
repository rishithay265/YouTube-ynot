# Gita Jayanti Shorts: English voice-over scripts (all 18)

Structure for every Short:

1. **HOOK**: a question, spoken over the music.
2. **BRIDGE**: "Krishna answers this in the Bhagavad Gita…" (Day 1 differs, see there).
3. **[CHANT]**: the Sanskrit verse, sung by Vāgdhenu. The voice-over is silent here and the music carries on.
4. **TRANSLATION**: faithful to Gita Press (Hindi translation, numbering as in Gita Press).
5. **IN SIMPLE WORDS**: one plain line.
6. **REFLECTION**: one or two lines, and no claims beyond the verse.

For each Short, record **PART A** (hook and bridge) and **PART B** (translation through reflection) as **two separate voice-only files**, with no music. The music bed and the chant are laid in between here. Target: Part A about 8–10 s, Part B about 18–22 s.

## Day 1: 1.1 (Dhritarashtra speaks)
**PART A**
The Bhagavad Gita does not begin with Krishna.
It begins with a blind king, asking a question he is afraid to hear answered.
**PART B**
"On the holy field of Kurukshetra, gathered and eager to fight — O Sanjaya, what did my sons and the sons of Pandu do?"
In simple words: a father asks what happened on the battlefield, and he asks it as "my sons" against "Pandu's sons".
Seven hundred verses of wisdom begin with a single word: *mine*.

## Day 2: 2.47
**PART A**
Why do we work so hard, and still feel empty?
Krishna answers this in the Bhagavad Gita, chapter 2, verse 47.
**PART B**
"Your right is to action alone, never to its fruits. Let not the fruit be your motive, and do not cling to inaction."
In simple words: do your work fully, and let go of the result.
The result was never in your hands. The effort always was.

## Day 3: 3.35
**PART A**
Why does living someone else's life feel so frightening?
Krishna answers this in the Bhagavad Gita, chapter 3, verse 35.
**PART B**
"Better is one's own dharma, even imperfectly done, than another's dharma done well. Better is death in one's own dharma; another's dharma brings fear."
In simple words: your own path, walked badly, is better than someone else's path, walked perfectly.
Comparison feels like ambition. Krishna calls it fear.

## Day 4: 4.7
**PART A**
What happens when evil starts winning?
When truth is ignored, and adharma rises everywhere?
Krishna answers this in the Bhagavad Gita, chapter 4, verse 7.
**PART B**
"Whenever there is a decline of dharma, O Bharata, and a rise of adharma, then I manifest Myself."
In simple words: when righteousness falls, the Divine does not stay silent.
Darkness may grow. It never gets the final word.

## Day 5: 5.18
**PART A**
How do you recognise a truly wise person?
Not by what they know, but by how they see.
Krishna answers this in the Bhagavad Gita, chapter 5, verse 18.
**PART B**
"The wise see with an equal eye a learned and humble Brahmin, a cow, an elephant, a dog, and one who eats dogs."
In simple words: the wise see the same Self in every living being.
Labels are for the eyes. Wisdom looks past them.

## Day 6: 6.5
**PART A**
Who is your greatest enemy?
The answer is closer than you think.
Krishna answers this in the Bhagavad Gita, chapter 6, verse 5.
**PART B**
"Lift yourself by your own self; do not let yourself sink. For the self alone is the friend of the self, and the self alone is its enemy."
In simple words: you can be your own best friend, or your own worst enemy.
No one else can pull you down as far, or lift you as high.

## Day 7: 7.19
**PART A**
After countless lifetimes, what does the wise soul finally understand?
Krishna answers this in the Bhagavad Gita, chapter 7, verse 19.
**PART B**
"At the end of many births, the one who has knowledge surrenders to Me, realising: Vasudeva is everything. Such a great soul is very rare."
In simple words: the deepest wisdom is to see the Divine in all that is.
It takes many lives to learn. Krishna says few ever do.

## Day 8: 8.7
**PART A**
At the very end of your life, what will you remember?
Krishna answers this in the Bhagavad Gita, chapter 8, verse 7.
**PART B**
"Therefore, at all times, remember Me, and fight. With your mind and intellect offered to Me, you will surely come to Me."
In simple words: keep the Divine in mind, and still do your duty.
Krishna does not say stop fighting. He says remember Me while you fight.

## Day 9: 9.26
**PART A**
What does God really want from you?
Not gold. Not grand rituals.
Krishna answers this in the Bhagavad Gita, chapter 9, verse 26.
**PART B**
"Whoever offers Me with devotion a leaf, a flower, a fruit, or water, that offering of love from a pure heart, I accept."
In simple words: even the smallest offering, given with love, reaches the Divine.
What is offered matters less than the heart that offers it.

## Day 10: 10.20
**PART A**
Where does God live?
In a temple? In the sky? Krishna gives a closer answer.
Bhagavad Gita, chapter 10, verse 20.
**PART B**
"I am the Self, O Gudakesha, seated in the hearts of all beings. I am the beginning, the middle, and the end of all beings."
In simple words: the Divine lives within every heart, from first breath to last.
You do not have to search far. Only deep.

## Day 11: 11.32
**PART A**
The most terrifying words in the Bhagavad Gita.
Arjuna sees Krishna's universal form, and asks: who are You?
The answer, chapter 11, verse 32.
**PART B**
"I am Time, the mighty destroyer of worlds, risen here to consume these people. Even without you, none of the warriors standing in the opposing armies will survive."
In simple words: Time takes everything. The outcome was decided before the battle began.
Arjuna is not the destroyer. He is only asked to be the instrument.

## Day 12: 12.13
**PART A**
What kind of person is truly dear to God?
Krishna answers this in the Bhagavad Gita, chapter 12, verse 13.
**PART B**
"One who hates no being, who is friendly and compassionate to all, free from 'mine' and from ego, equal in pleasure and pain, and forgiving."
In simple words: no hatred, kindness to all, no ego, and a forgiving heart.
In the very next verse, Krishna says: such a devotee is dear to Me.

## Day 13: 13.2
**PART A**
Krishna says your body is a field.
So who is the one who knows the field?
Bhagavad Gita, chapter 13, verse 2.
**PART B**
"Know Me also as the knower of the field in all fields, O Bharata. The knowledge of the field and of its knower, that, in My view, is true knowledge."
In simple words: the body is the field; the one who knows it is the Divine within.
To know the difference is what Krishna calls real knowledge.

## Day 14: 14.5
**PART A**
What are the three invisible ropes that bind every soul?
Krishna answers this in the Bhagavad Gita, chapter 14, verse 5.
**PART B**
"Sattva, rajas and tamas, these three qualities born of nature, O mighty-armed one, bind the imperishable soul to the body."
In simple words: goodness, passion and inertia all tie the soul to the body.
Even goodness is a rope. A golden one, but still a rope.

## Day 15: 15.7
**PART A**
Who are you, really?
Not your name. Not your body.
Krishna answers this in the Bhagavad Gita, chapter 15, verse 7.
**PART B**
"An eternal part of Me has become the living being in this world of life. It draws to itself the mind and the five senses, which rest in nature."
In simple words: your soul is an eternal part of the Divine.
The mind and senses are what it carries. They are not what it is.

## Day 16: 16.21
**PART A**
Krishna names three gates that lead straight to hell.
Do you know what they are?
Bhagavad Gita, chapter 16, verse 21.
**PART B**
"Desire, anger and greed: these are the three gates of hell, the destroyers of the self. Therefore, one should give up these three."
In simple words: lust, anger and greed destroy you from within.
Every fall begins at one of these three gates.

## Day 17: 17.20
**PART A**
What is true charity?
Krishna answers this in the Bhagavad Gita, chapter 17, verse 20.
**PART B**
"A gift given simply because giving is right, to one who cannot return it, at the proper place and time, and to a worthy person, that gift is called sattvic."
In simple words: give because it is right, not because you expect something back.
The purest gift is the one that asks for nothing in return.

## Day 18: 18.66 (Gita Jayanti)
**PART A**
After seven hundred verses, what is Krishna's final word?
Bhagavad Gita, chapter 18, verse 66.
**PART B**
"Setting aside all dharmas, take refuge in Me alone. I will free you from all sins. Do not grieve."
In simple words: let go of every support you lean on, and surrender to the Divine. Krishna takes the burden.
The Gita ends not with a command, but with a promise: do not grieve.

*("Setting aside all dharmas" is the literal sense. Gita Press glosses it as offering all duties to Him, not as abandoning action, so the script avoids "stop doing your duty".)*
